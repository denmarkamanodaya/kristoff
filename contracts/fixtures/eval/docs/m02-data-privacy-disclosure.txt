NATIONAL PRIVACY TRIBUNAL (FICTIONAL), CASE NO. NPT-0002
CCC (complainant) v. DDD LENDING CORPORATION (respondent)

DECISION

The complainant CCC borrowed money through the mobile application of the respondent DDD
Lending Corporation. When CCC fell behind on payments, collection agents of the respondent
sent messages to at least thirty (30) persons in CCC's phone contact list, stating that CCC
was a fraudster and attaching CCC's photo and identification card.

The respondent argued that CCC consented to access to her contacts when she installed the
application.

<<<PAGE BREAK>>>

RULING

Consent to access a contact list for identity verification is not consent to disclose the
borrower's personal information to those contacts for collection. The disclosure had no
lawful purpose and was made without the consent of the data subject.

The Tribunal finds the respondent liable for unauthorized disclosure under Section 32 of
Republic Act No. 10173, the Data Privacy Act of 2012, and recommends prosecution of the
responsible officers. The respondent is ordered to stop all third-party collection
messages and to delete the contact data it collected.

SO ORDERED.
